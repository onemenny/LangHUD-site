LangHUD is a small menu bar app for people who type in more than one language. It tells you which keyboard language is on as you start typing, and turns text typed in the wrong one into what you meant, with one key press.

Private by design: your text is read on your Mac and never sent anywhere. Not a keylogger.

How it works

A quiet helper, right where you type

LangHUD sits in the menu bar and speaks up only when it matters.

  1. 1

    Install and allow Accessibility

    Drag LangHUD into Applications and turn on its Accessibility permission, which lets it see the text field you are in.

  2. 2

    Start typing

    When you click into a field, switch apps or come back after a pause, LangHUD says the keyboard language out loud and shows it in a small card.

  3. 3

    Typed in the wrong one? Fix it

    Select the text and press the hot key. LangHUD converts it to the other layout and switches your keyboard to match.

Features

Two things LangHUD does best

Fix text typed in the wrong layout

Typed a whole sentence in Hebrew letters when you meant English? Select it and press the hot key. LangHUD converts the selected text to the other keyboard layout and switches your keyboard, so you can keep typing. Press again to convert it back.

יקךךם 'םרךג → hello world

Hear which language you are typing in

A short spoken cue says the keyboard language, for example "English" or "Hebrew", when you start typing, switch apps or come back after a pause. You know without looking, and can turn it off any time.

🔈 "English"

On-screen reminder

A small card shows the language as you start typing, then fades away.

Language in the menu bar

The menu bar icon shows the current layout, such as EN or ע, at a glance.

Private, and not a keylogger

Text is read on your Mac only and never sent anywhere. Your typing, your text and the apps you use are never sent, and LangHUD does not ask for Input Monitoring.

FAQ

Questions and answers

Is LangHUD a keylogger?

No. LangHUD does not record what you type and does not ask for the Input Monitoring permission that keyloggers need. It looks at the text field you are working in only to notice that you started typing and to convert text you select, and keeps nothing.

Does my text leave my Mac?

Never. Your typing, your text and the apps you use are never sent: no account, no analytics, no crash reports. LangHUD sends a license check (key, activation id, app version), asks langhud.app for updates, and sends a feedback report only when you write one and press Send.

Why does it need the Accessibility permission?

macOS lets an app see which text field you are in, and replace the text you selected, only with Accessibility. LangHUD explains this on first launch and opens the right page of System Settings for you. Without it, the menu bar icon still works.

How do I fix text typed in the wrong language?

Select the text, then press the hot key shown in LangHUD's Settings. The selection is converted to your other keyboard layout and the keyboard switches to match. Nothing selected means nothing changes.

Can I turn the spoken cue off?

Yes. The spoken cue, the on-screen reminder and the hot key each have their own switch in Settings.

Does it work in every app?

In most apps, yes. A few apps draw their own text in a way macOS cannot describe to other apps; LangHUD lists the ones it knows about in its Known Limitations window.

Which Macs are supported?

Any Mac, Apple silicon or Intel, running macOS 13 Ventura or later, with two or more keyboard languages turned on.

How much does it cost?

The HUD is free. There is a 14-day trial of the paid features (the spoken cue and text correction), then $10 one-time. One license covers up to 3 of your Macs, with free updates through 1.x.

1. Install

  1. Open LangHUD.dmg from your Downloads folder.
  2. Drag LangHUD onto the Applications folder in the window that opens.

2. Open it the first time

  1. Open LangHUD from Applications. macOS says it cannot verify the developer; close the message.
  2. Open System Settings > Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway next to LangHUD.
  3. Confirm with Open (you may be asked for your password).

On older versions of macOS: right-click (or Control-click) LangHUD in Applications, choose Open, then Open again.

3. Turn on Accessibility

  1. On first launch LangHUD explains why it needs Accessibility and offers to open System Settings.
  2. In System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility, turn on LangHUD.

After installing an update, if LangHUD stops noticing your typing, turn its Accessibility switch off and on again.
